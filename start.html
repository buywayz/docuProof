Frequently Asked Questions

Why would an everyday person need this?⌄
You'd be surprised how often "prove it" comes up in everyday life. Landlords disputing move-in photos. Contractors blaming you for damage they caused. Insurance companies claiming your policy "changed." Family members fighting over a will. A $2.89 timestamp today can save you thousands in disputes tomorrow. See 9 real scenarios where proof saves the day →
Is this a legal notary service?⌄
No. docuProof creates cryptographic proof-of-existence, not legal notarization. It provides mathematical evidence that your file existed at a specific time and hasn't changed since—evidence that can support legal claims but doesn't replace formal notarization where required.
Does my file get uploaded anywhere?⌄
No. Your file never leaves your device. We hash it locally in your browser and only submit the 64-character SHA-256 hash — a unique cryptographic code derived from your file. Your document's contents remain completely private — we never see them.
What exactly gets stored on the blockchain?⌄
Only your file's cryptographic hash — a unique 64-character code derived from your file. This hash cannot be reversed to reconstruct your file — it's a one-way mathematical function. Think of it as a unique digital seal that proves the file existed.
How long does blockchain anchoring take?⌄
Blockchain anchoring typically completes within 1–4 hours as transactions are batched for efficiency. You'll immediately receive a receipt with your hash and Proof ID by email. Once anchoring is confirmed, you'll receive a second email with your Certificate of Proof, including the blockchain block address. If you added RIP (Redundant Identity Preservation), the certificate will include your RIP verification as well.
What should I save after generating a proof?⌄
Keep two things: (1) Your original file, unchanged, and (2) The PDF certificate you receive. Together, these allow anyone to independently verify your proof at any time—even if docuProof disappears.
Can I verify my proof without docuProof?⌄
Yes. Your certificate contains everything needed for independent verification using the OpenTimestamps protocol. docuProof is not a single point of failure—the proof exists on the blockchain regardless of our service.
Why the Bitcoin blockchain instead of others?⌄
The Bitcoin blockchain is the most secure, decentralized, and time-tested blockchain in existence. It's been running continuously since 2009 and has the highest hashrate of any blockchain, making it virtually impossible to tamper with historical records.
What happens if I lose my original file?⌄
Without your original file, you can't prove your claim—the timestamp only proves some file existed. After checkout, we offer RIP (Redundant Identity Preservation) to verify your backup copies match your original, protecting you if one copy is lost.
What can accidentally change my file?⌄
Opening and re-saving, re-exporting, or software updating metadata—even without visible changes. Renaming is safe (it doesn't change contents). Best practice: make backup copies right after timestamping and don't open them.
How reliable is the file verification?⌄
We use SHA-256—the same algorithm securing the blockchain and banking systems. Matching hashes means files are mathematically identical, bit for bit. It's not an estimate; it's cryptographic certainty.
Is docuProof only for businesses?⌄
Not at all. Homeowners and families use docuProof just as much—rental move-in photos, contractor quotes, home inventory for insurance, car condition before lending, even custody agreements. A $2.89 timestamp can save thousands in disputes. See 10 things smart individuals timestamp →
What documents should I timestamp?⌄
Anything where proving "when it existed" might matter later. For businesses: contracts, approvals, quotes, NDAs, creative work. For individuals: rental photos, contractor agreements, valuables documentation, receipts. Business use cases → · Personal use cases →
What is a cryptographic hash, and how is it connected to the Proof ID?⌄
A cryptographic hash is a unique 64-character code generated from your file using SHA-256 — the same algorithm securing the blockchain and banking systems. If even a single byte of your file changes, the hash changes entirely. The hash proves your file is authentic (unchanged), while the Proof ID helps you find and share your proof quickly.
